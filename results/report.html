The put ladder from 2020: yearly returns, drawdown and leverage

One continuous account from 1 Jan 2020 to today, compounding. Every month (30.4 days) a 1-year ATM put is sold with a notional of leverage × current equity ÷ 12. When a put has earned 95% of its premium, it is bought back to free margin. Premiums follow the market: 1-year IV is half of Deribit's 30-day DVOL plus 26 points, so DVOL 56 gives 54, today's quote. Before DVOL starts in Mar 2021, realised volatility stands in for it. ETH futures are assumed to trade 5% a year above spot, and puts are sold 1.5% below their value. The model's 1-year ATM put premium by year: 2020: 25% · 2021: 30% · 2022: 26% · 2023: 18% · 2024: 21% · 2025: 22% · 2026: 20%.

Margin follows Deribit's standard rules for short options with USD collateral. To open a new put you need 15% of the ETH price plus the option's value. To keep it open you need 7.5% plus the option's value. If the account drops below that, everything is bought back and the run stops. "Leverage" is the total put notional divided by the account value when each put is sold. The size-up is your rule B (an extra tranche whose premium equals the last loss), capped at 3× the normal tranche. "4% yield" means cash collateral earns 4% a year.

How to lever it

  1. Make the collateral earn first. Cash-secured puts leave the whole notional idle. Earning about 4% on it added roughly 4–5 points a year without adding any ETH risk. In practice that means either collateral that earns yield on your venue, or keeping only the margin (about 35% of notional at 1×) on the exchange and the rest in a stablecoin lending pool, topped up when ETH falls. The second route needs a strict top-up plan, because a late top-up means liquidation.
  2. Keep your size-up rule, capped at 3×. It adds exposure right after a crash, when premiums are richest. That added about 3 points a year and shortened the recovery from the 2022 drawdown by almost a year.
  3. Raise notional only as far as you can stomach the drop. 1× had about a −42% worst drop, 1.5× about −60%, 2× about −78%. Above about 2.5× a 2022-style crash liquidates the account. Deribit's opening margin already caps naked ATM puts near 2.9×.
  4. Skip put spreads and trend filters for this strategy. In this history both lowered returns more than they lowered drawdowns.

Yearly puts only, laddered monthly, sized up to cover losses

Sell the ETH and keep the dollars as capital. Every month, sell a 1-year ATM put with a notional of 1/12 of the capital. When a put expires, pay the strike minus the price if it is in the money, then sell a new 1-year put at the current price. After a losing tranche (payout larger than premium), the replacement is sized up only enough to cover that loss:

  • Rule A: the new tranche's whole premium equals the loss, never smaller than the base. Example: ETH −60%, premium 22%, loss 38% → 38/22 = 1.7× the base.
  • Rule B: the base tranche plus an extra one whose premium equals the loss. Same example → 1 + 1.7 = 2.7×.

Tranches are sized in dollars, so a rally does not inflate them. Sized in ETH, windows that start cheap, such as 2018–2020, end up selling puts worth many times the starting money; that version lost heavily for those start years. Yearly straddles instead of ETH, laddered monthly, with recovery sizing

No ETH is held. Every month, sell a 1-year ATM put and call (a straddle) on 4.17 ETH (50 ÷ 12) and collect 44% of the price for both legs, less fees. When a tranche expires, pay size × |price − strike| in cash and sell a new 1-year straddle at the current price. Your rule for the size after a losing tranche (loss = payout − premium) can be read two ways, so both are tested:

  • Rule A: the new tranche is sized so that half of its premium equals the loss, never smaller than the base. In your example (move 60%, premium 44%, loss 16%), half of a normal tranche's premium (22%) already covers 16%, so the size stays 1×. It grows only for moves above about 66%.
  • Rule B: keep the base tranche and add an extra one whose half-premium covers the loss. In your example that is 1 + 16/22 = 1.73×.

Open straddles are valued each month at the implied volatility the premium implies (44% → 56%). The lowest point of cash minus that value is the money you would need to keep the position open. Monthly ladder of yearly calls on 50 ETH

Every month on the 12th, sell a 1-year ATM call on 4.17 ETH (50 ÷ 12). In the first year coverage builds from 1/12 to 12/12. From then on, each month one call expires and a new one is sold at the current price. If the expiring call ended in the money, you pay the difference in cash and keep the ETH. If it ended out of the money, you keep the whole premium. The ETH is never sold. Calls still open at the end are bought back at their value that day; that is the fair cost of stopping. Their value is priced with the volatility the premium implies: 66% for 26%, 51% for 20%.

Weekly calls: summary

  • It is slow. With ATM strikes you are fully out after about 65 weeks (median). With +5% strikes only 4% of paths are out within two years, and with +10% strikes you still hold about 46 ETH after two years. Until then you are long the unsold ETH, so the result mostly follows the ETH price.
  • The premium improves your sale price; it is not income. Since 2021, weekly calls paid back almost exactly what they collected: -0.1% of premium at ATM, -2.9% at +5%, +0.1% at +10%, after spread and fees. Over a full ATM exit you collect about $6,803 and keep −$38.
  • Without a floor you sell into dips. The strike follows the price down, so in a fall you sell cheap. If ETH dips to about $1,200 and comes back to $2,000, ATM calls end −19% against selling today.
  • A $2,000 strike floor fixes that. Use strike = the higher of ATM (or +5%) and $2,000, so you never sell below $2,000. The same dip then ends +13% (2 ETH a week) or +14% (5 ETH a week at +5%). A range around $2,000 gives +15% and +17%.
  • The cost is that you may not get out. In a 2022-style slide nothing sells: you end −38% against selling today, about the same as holding (−36%), with 60 ETH left. If you need to be out for certain, sell today.

Your rule sells on every up day, whatever the price. Daily ETH options are not cheap to sell into. How to use this

If you exit with weekly calls

  1. Set the strike to the higher of ATM (or +5%) and $2,000. This one rule decides whether the ladder sells high or sells into dips.
  2. Pick the size by how fast you want out. At 2 ETH a week, expect well over a year even if ETH holds up. 5 ETH a week roughly doubles the pace.
  3. Treat the unsold ETH as still at risk. In a slide you keep holding, and the result is close to just holding. Decide in advance at what price you would sell the rest outright.
  4. Hedge exactly at the Friday 08:00 UTC settlement, and only when the call ends in the money. Short 2 ETH-PERP or sell 2 ETH of wstETH; the economics are the same, but the perp keeps staking running and needs margin.
  5. Sell the calls at mid where you can. Weekly ATM spreads are about 0.001 ETH, roughly 3% of the premium.

If you run the 1 ETH a day plan

  1. Treat it as holding ETH with a range-trading overlay, not as an exit. It pays when ETH keeps coming back to about $2,000 and costs you when ETH trends away.
  2. Keep the $2,000 floor. It stops the plan from selling the rebound after a dip, which was the main flaw of the version without it.
  3. Hold USD in reserve: roughly $35–40k per 1 ETH a day of lot size, and up to $75–125k in a bad stretch. In a 2022-style slide, plan for $55k (up to $128k).
  4. Keep margin for the perp shorts on the exchange. In a rally they lose cash every day while your wstETH gains are on-chain.
  5. Scale only as far as your cash allows. 2–3 ETH a day multiplies both the range gain and the tail roughly in proportion.
  6. Execution: post at mid as a maker rather than hitting the bid. Trade at the 08:00 UTC settlement and convert the ETH-paid premium to USD.

If you want out

  1. Sell the 50 wstETH now. That locks $125,411.
  2. Or get paid to wait a day or two with calls only and a stop. Sell 62.45 ETH of the next daily ATM call. On the first up settlement, hedge and sell. If any settlement is at or below $1,909, sell everything. Historically the median was +1.1% (about +$1,370) and the worst 5% was −6.2% (about −$7,797).
  3. Today is a worse-than-average day to start. ETH is −10% in four days. Starts after drops like this had a worst 5% of −12.1% for the quick exit, against −6.0% otherwise.

Assumptions and limits

Position: 50 wstETH × 1.2491 = 62.45 ETH; options and hedges sized in ETH. One daily expiry per day at 08:00 UTC, strike = settlement price, Black–Scholes pricing. IV = DVOL × weekday factor (Mon–Thu 1.00, Fri 0.92, Sat 0.60, Sun 0.85, shaped on tonight's Deribit term structure); day one uses the live 61.0% ATM IV. Options sold 3.5% below mark, fee 0.03% of underlying per leg (capped at 12.5% of premium). Perp hedge 0.03% per change; shorts receive 1.5% APR funding in simulations and actual monthly funding in the backtest. Staking 2.33% APR (backtest uses the historical Lido rate by year). Extra wstETH costs 0.03% to buy; the final sale costs 0.05%. No yield on USD on either side. Monte Carlo resamples ETH's 2021–2026 daily 08:00 moves by weekday, scaled to each scenario's vol, so fat tails and quiet weekends are kept.

Not modelled: taxes, gas, margin calls or liquidation, wstETH discount to ETH, exchange risk, and moves between settlements (the hedge is placed only at 08:00). This is a simulation of your rules, not financial advice.
