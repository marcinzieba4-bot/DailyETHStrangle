Yearly calls on 50 ETH, ETH never sold

Every November, sell a 1-year ATM call on 50 ETH and receive 26% of the price in USD, less a 0.03% fee. At expiry, pay 50 × (price − strike) if ETH finished above the strike, then sell the next call. The 50 ETH are never sold; staking is the same as for holding, so it is left out. The running cash column shows what you would have to fund from elsewhere to keep the ETH untouched.

Weekly calls: summary

  • It is slow. With ATM strikes you are fully out after about 65 weeks (median). With +5% strikes only 4% of paths are out within two years, and with +10% strikes you still hold about 46 ETH after two years. Until then you are long the unsold ETH, so the result mostly follows the ETH price.
  • The premium improves your sale price; it is not income. Since 2021, weekly calls paid back almost exactly what they collected: -0.1% of premium at ATM, -2.9% at +5%, +0.1% at +10%, after spread and fees. Over a full ATM exit you collect about $6,803 and keep −$38.
  • Without a floor you sell into dips. The strike follows the price down, so in a fall you sell cheap. If ETH dips to about $1,200 and comes back to $2,000, ATM calls end −19% against selling today.
  • A $2,000 strike floor fixes that. Use strike = the higher of ATM (or +5%) and $2,000, so you never sell below $2,000. The same dip then ends +13% (2 ETH a week) or +14% (5 ETH a week at +5%). A range around $2,000 gives +15% and +17%.
  • The cost is that you may not get out. In a 2022-style slide nothing sells: you end −38% against selling today, about the same as holding (−36%), with 60 ETH left. If you need to be out for certain, sell today.

Weekly calls: 2 ETH a week, hedge only when the call ends in the money

Every Friday at the 08:00 UTC settlement, sell a weekly call on 2 ETH, or 5 ETH in one variant. At expiry, if ETH is above the strike, pay the difference and short 2 ETH-PERP. That locks those 2 ETH at the strike, while the wstETH keeps staking until you are fully hedged and sell. If ETH is below the strike, keep the premium. No puts, no buying. So the "mean reversion" this plan needs is a view about ETH's price level, such as "it will be back above $2,000". That is a bullish bet. The plan doubles down on it as the price falls.

The fix: sell only above your line, and cap the buying

Your rule sells on every up day, whatever the price. In the dip-then-recover scenario, capping the position at 125 ETH without a price floor sells the whole rebound at a median of $1,527 per ETH and ends at −22%. Selling calls only while ETH is at or above $2,000 turns the same scenario into +19%. That version needs at most about $186,483 of extra USD (worst 5%). It sells out at a median of $3,062, but only 29% of the time within a year. It averages +3% with no view and +21% if ETH mean-reverts. In history its 1-year median was +39% (mean +35%), but the worst 5% ended at −104% or worse: holding 2× ETH through a crash is still holding 2× ETH.

Why there is no free lunch here

Daily ETH options are not cheap to sell into. Weekend days are already priced lower by the market, and the realised 08:00 move has been about 93% of what the option paid before costs (a 6.9% gross edge). Selling at the bid and paying 0.03% per leg removes most of that. The seller won on 63% of days, but the worst single day cost 21.5% of spot.

How to use this

If you exit with weekly calls

  1. Set the strike to the higher of ATM (or +5%) and $2,000. This one rule decides whether the ladder sells high or sells into dips.
  2. Pick the size by how fast you want out. At 2 ETH a week, expect well over a year even if ETH holds up. 5 ETH a week roughly doubles the pace.
  3. Treat the unsold ETH as still at risk. In a slide you keep holding, and the result is close to just holding. Decide in advance at what price you would sell the rest outright.
  4. Hedge exactly at the Friday 08:00 UTC settlement, and only when the call ends in the money. Short 2 ETH-PERP or sell 2 ETH of wstETH; the economics are the same, but the perp keeps staking running and needs margin.
  5. Sell the calls at mid where you can. Weekly ATM spreads are about 0.001 ETH, roughly 3% of the premium.

If you run the 1 ETH a day plan

  1. Treat it as holding ETH with a range-trading overlay, not as an exit. It pays when ETH keeps coming back to about $2,000 and costs you when ETH trends away.
  2. Keep the $2,000 floor. It stops the plan from selling the rebound after a dip, which was the main flaw of the version without it.
  3. Hold USD in reserve: roughly $35–40k per 1 ETH a day of lot size, and up to $75–125k in a bad stretch. In a 2022-style slide, plan for $55k (up to $128k).
  4. Keep margin for the perp shorts on the exchange. In a rally they lose cash every day while your wstETH gains are on-chain.
  5. Scale only as far as your cash allows. 2–3 ETH a day multiplies both the range gain and the tail roughly in proportion.
  6. Execution: post at mid as a maker rather than hitting the bid. Trade at the 08:00 UTC settlement and convert the ETH-paid premium to USD.

If you want out

  1. Sell the 50 wstETH now. That locks $125,411.
  2. Or get paid to wait a day or two with calls only and a stop. Sell 62.45 ETH of the next daily ATM call. On the first up settlement, hedge and sell. If any settlement is at or below $1,909, sell everything. Historically the median was +1.1% (about +$1,370) and the worst 5% was −6.2% (about −$7,797).
  3. Today is a worse-than-average day to start. ETH is −10% in four days. Starts after drops like this had a worst 5% of −12.1% for the quick exit, against −6.0% otherwise.

Assumptions and limits

Position: 50 wstETH × 1.2491 = 62.45 ETH; options and hedges sized in ETH. One daily expiry per day at 08:00 UTC, strike = settlement price, Black–Scholes pricing. IV = DVOL × weekday factor (Mon–Thu 1.00, Fri 0.92, Sat 0.60, Sun 0.85, shaped on tonight's Deribit term structure); day one uses the live 61.0% ATM IV. Options sold 3.5% below mark, fee 0.03% of underlying per leg (capped at 12.5% of premium). Perp hedge 0.03% per change; shorts receive 1.5% APR funding in simulations and actual monthly funding in the backtest. Staking 2.33% APR (backtest uses the historical Lido rate by year). Extra wstETH costs 0.03% to buy; the final sale costs 0.05%. No yield on USD on either side. Monte Carlo resamples ETH's 2021–2026 daily 08:00 moves by weekday, scaled to each scenario's vol, so fat tails and quiet weekends are kept.

Not modelled: taxes, gas, margin calls or liquidation, wstETH discount to ETH, exchange risk, and moves between settlements (the hedge is placed only at 08:00). This is a simulation of your rules, not financial advice.
